Darts S.A.M, Autodarts and DartCounter compared

Three ways to keep score at your own board. What each one is, what it costs, and where they differ. What is said here about Autodarts and DartCounter is from their own websites, with a link to where they say it.

Autodarts

Autodarts is a German darts platform built around automatic scoring (autodarts.com). It scores in two ways. Lens uses the camera of a phone or tablet pointed at the board. Hardware autoscoring uses cameras fixed round the board: their own Autodarts X, with four HD cameras and a light ring built in, or cameras you put up yourself with their desktop app on Windows, macOS or Linux (system requirements).

The free plan includes five Lens matches a month, core statistics, a voice caller, online tournaments and eleven games. Autodarts Plus is £5.99 a month or £59.99 a year, and adds unlimited Lens and two more games, Killer and 121 Checkout (autodarts.com). You can play the bot, friends, or strangers online through matchmaking and tournaments, in the browser or the iPhone and Android apps. They say more than 200,000 people play on it.

DartCounter

DartCounter is a darts scoring app for matches, practice and statistics (dartcounter.net), on iPhone, Android and in the browser. Its strength is playing other people online: the free plan has three online matches a week, and DartCounter Ultimate (a subscription; its price isn't on their website) unlocks unlimited online games, fuller statistics and ranked matches (what Ultimate includes). They say more than 1.5 million people play on it (dartcounter.net).

DartCounter doesn't score by camera itself: you enter your scores. A ranked match needs a camera so your opponent can see your board, and if you own a Target Omni (a camera scoring system made by Target) it can score for you (DartCounter Ranked).

Where Darts S.A.M is different

  • Everything is free. Camera scoring, all fourteen games, the stats and the TV view. There is no paid plan and there are no adverts. They cover every game Autodarts lists, including Killer and 121 Checkout, which are on its paid plan (Round the World is a choice inside Around the Clock).
  • It's open source. All of the code is on GitHub under the AGPL-3.0: you can read how it scores, change it, or run your own copy of the whole site.
  • It uses webcams you can buy for a few pounds each. Two or three USB webcams round the board and a Mac, Windows or Linux computer. How it works and what to buy.
  • The scores go on every screen you have. Score at the board and follow along on a phone, a tablet or the telly, with a TV view laid out like a match on television.

Where the others are ahead

  • Playing people online. Both have matchmaking, tournaments and big communities to play against. Darts S.A.M is for the people round your own board, plus a bot to practise against.
  • Scoring with just a phone. Autodarts Lens scores from a phone's camera today. Darts S.A.M has only an early beta of this so far; its camera scoring needs webcams and a computer.
  • A finished, boxed product. Autodarts X comes ready to fit. With Darts S.A.M you choose the webcams, mount them and set it up yourself, and it is younger: what has been tested so far is written down openly.

If you want online matches against strangers, DartCounter or Autodarts is the better choice. If you want camera scoring at home that costs nothing, has no adverts and that you can see inside, try Darts S.A.M. Tapping the board works on any phone, with no cameras at all.
